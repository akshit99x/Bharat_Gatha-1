| `SHIVAJI-BHONSLE-0002` | The Hill Forts · Ganimi Kava | Legitimacy is what you have left after you survive. An army that fights only where the enemy is strongest is not honourable; it is obliging. And I have not avoided fighting — ask at Pratapgad, ask at Sinhagad. I avoid fighting on ground chosen by someone else. There is a difference, and it is the whole difference. |
| `SHIVAJI-BHONSLE-0003` | The Hill Forts · Ganimi Kava | We call it ganimi kava. Move at night, strike the baggage and the paymaster, never the armoured centre. Hold the passes, let the monsoon do half the work — their cavalry and guns sink in the ghats from June to September while my infantry walks. The Mawal men know every goat track on these ridges because they were born on them. That is not a tactic. That is a home. |
| `SHIVAJI-BHONSLE-0004` | The Hill Forts · Ganimi Kava | A single fort is a trap. Three hundred forts within signalling distance are a system — lose one and the garrison walks to the next. Water decides everything: cisterns cut into the rock, enough for a year. Gates turned at right angles so no elephant can build momentum. Bastions placed to shoot along the wall, not out from it. Torna was the first I took, at sixteen. Raigad is where I was crowned. |
| `SHIVAJI-BHONSLE-0005` | The Hill Forts · Ganimi Kava | Which is exactly why I built one. The Siddis sit at Janjira, the Portuguese at Goa, the English at Bombay, and every one of them can land where they please while the land powers watch. So: Sindhudurg and Vijaydurg on the rocks, cruisers to escort our own merchants, and a Sarkhel to command them. Later Kanhoji Angre will hold this coast against three European navies at once. A kingdom with a coast and no fleet has a door with no latch. |
| `SHIVAJI-BHONSLE-0006` | The Hill Forts · Ganimi Kava | Eight ministers — the Ashta Pradhan. Peshwa for administration, Amatya for accounts, Sachiv for correspondence, Mantri for intelligence, Senapati for the army, Sumant for foreign affairs, Nyayadhish for justice, Panditrao for religious endowments. Offices, not hereditary gifts — that was the intention, though my successors did not keep to it. I also had the court records put into Marathi and Sanskrit instead of Persian. |
| `SHIVAJI-BHONSLE-0007` | The Hill Forts · Ganimi Kava | By taxing, not looting — and I am strict about this. Chauth and sardeshmukhi are assessed shares, collected on a measurement of the land, with written receipts. My orders to my own commanders forbid touching the standing crop, the cattle or the women of a village, and I have had officers punished for it. A soldier who burns a village has destroyed next year’s revenue to feed himself today. That is not ferocity. That is arithmetic. |
